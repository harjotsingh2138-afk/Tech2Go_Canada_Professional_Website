What To Do When Your Commercial Truck Breaks Down on the Highway in Ontario

There's nothing worse than feeling your rig start to lose power on the 401 at 3am with a load to deliver. But staying calm and taking the right steps can mean the difference between a 45-minute fix and a multi-hour ordeal.

Here's exactly what to do when your commercial truck breaks down on an Ontario highway.

Step 1: Get to Safety First

As soon as you notice something is wrong — strange noises, warning lights, loss of power — activate your four-way hazard flashers immediately and begin safely moving to the right shoulder.

Do not stop in a live lane of traffic. If you can't make it to a full shoulder, get as far right as possible and stay in your cab with your seatbelt on until help arrives.

If you're on a 400-series highway, try to exit at the nearest interchange before stopping if you feel it's safe to do so.

Step 2: Make Your Truck Visible

Once stopped on the shoulder:

  • Keep your four-way flashers running at all times
  • Place road flares or reflective triangles at least 30 meters behind your vehicle
  • If it's dark or low visibility, use your flashlight to flag traffic if needed
  • Never stand between the road and your truck

Step 3: Call for Roadside Assistance Immediately

Time is money. Don't sit and wait — call a mobile mechanic the moment you're safely stopped.

Have this information ready when you call:

  • Your exact location (highway name, kilometer marker, nearest exit)
  • What symptoms your truck is showing
  • Your truck make, model, and year
  • What load you're carrying (if relevant)

Step 4: Stay in Your Cab (Unless It's Unsafe)

Ontario highways move fast. The safest place to wait is inside your cab, on the passenger side if possible, with your seatbelt on. If there's any risk of fire or the truck is in an unstable position, exit from the passenger door and move well away from the road.

Step 5: Notify Your Dispatcher and Receiver

Call your dispatcher to let them know about the breakdown and your estimated delay. Contact your receiver if the delivery timeline will be affected. Document everything for insurance purposes — take photos of the truck, the location, and any visible damage.

Common Causes of Commercial Truck Breakdowns

  • Tire blowouts — the #1 cause of roadside breakdowns. Check tire pressure before every run.
  • Brake issues — air leaks, worn pads, or overheated brakes. Listen for hissing sounds.
  • Electrical faults — dead batteries, alternator failure, or sensor issues triggering warning lights.
  • DPF/DEF system problems — increasingly common on newer diesel trucks. A clogged DPF can cause your truck to derate.
  • Fuel system issues — fuel filter clogs, contaminated fuel, or injector problems.
  • Overheating — often from a coolant leak or failing water pump.
