Billing Guide 🧾

One of the most common questions we get is simple: what's this going to cost me? Every job is different, but a few consistent factors shape the final invoice on any roadside call.

What's Included on a Typical Invoice

A roadside repair bill is generally made up of a service/trip charge for dispatching a mechanic to your location, an hourly labor charge for the actual repair work, and the cost of any parts used. Some calls may also include a time-of-day adjustment, described below.

What Drives the Price Up or Down

Time of day: Overnight, weekend, and holiday calls typically cost more than a weekday daytime call, since emergency dispatch works the same way most trades price after-hours work.

Distance to your location: A breakdown far from any service hub takes longer to reach, which factors into the trip charge.

Complexity of the repair: A quick tire change is priced differently than an extended diagnostic session for an intermittent fault.

Parts availability: Common parts are usually on hand; specialty parts that need to be sourced can add time to the job.

How to Avoid Surprises

  • Always ask for a total estimate before a mechanic is dispatched
  • Confirm whether the trip charge is separate from labor
  • Ask about after-hours adjustments up front
  • Get the final invoice in writing for your records
