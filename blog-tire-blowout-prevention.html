Tire Blowouts: What Causes Them and How to Prevent One

A blowout at highway speed is one of the most dangerous events a truck driver can face. Most are preventable.

Prevention 🛞

Tire blowouts rarely happen out of nowhere — heat buildup, underinflation, and wear combine over time until a tire finally fails, often at the worst possible moment on the highway.

What Actually Causes a Blowout

Underinflated tires flex more with every rotation, generating heat that weakens the internal structure. Overloading has the same effect. Add in road debris, curb damage, or simply an old tire past its usable life, and failure becomes a matter of when, not if.

Check Pressure Weekly, Not Monthly

Tire pressure drops with temperature changes and slow leaks you can't see. A weekly check with a proper gauge catches problems long before they become a roadside emergency.

Watch for Warning Signs

  • Uneven or rapid tread wear on one section of the tire
  • Visible bulges or sidewall cracking
  • Vibration that increases with speed
  • A tire that runs noticeably hotter than the others

Rotate and Replace on Schedule

Following a rotation schedule evens out wear across your tires, and replacing tires before they're fully worn (not after) is far cheaper than an emergency roadside call plus lost time.
